# Calibration targets

Generated by `make_calibration.py` (`uv run --with manifold3d --with numpy --with opencv-python-headless --with
matplotlib python hardware/calibration/make_calibration.py`), which also checks that OpenCV detects every corner of
both boards.

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `../prints/charuco_stereo_8x11_22mm.pdf` | Cameras' own calibration (intrinsics + left-right stereo). 8 x 11 squares of 22 mm (176 x 242 mm) on A4. Glue on foam board or a clipboard, hold by hand, cover the whole view at 0.4-0.8 m. Do this first. |
| `../prints/charuco_handeye_5x5_18mm.pdf` | Hand-eye calibration (head camera to robot base). 5 x 5 squares of 18 mm (90 x 90 mm), the dashed line is the backing plate outline. Mounted on the gripper with the clamp below. |
| `../prints/handeye_board_plate.stl` | 98 x 98 x 3 mm backing plate; two countersunk M3 holes, countersinks on the board side |
| `../prints/handeye_jaw_clamp.stl` | Sleeve clamp for the SO-101 fixed jaw with the plate flange (43.8 x 28 x 17 mm) |
| `handeye_assembly.png`, `handeye_clamp.png` | Renders: clamp on the jaw, plate in place |

Both boards: dictionary `DICT_4X4_100`, marker = 3/4 of the square; marker ids 0-43 (stereo) and 50-61 (hand-eye),
so the two boards are never confused. Print at 100 % / actual size, then measure a square with calipers and use the
measured size (the 50 mm bar on each page is a quick check). 600 dpi renders of the PDFs measure 17.992 and
22.014 mm squares.

## Hand-eye target assembly

Parts: the clamp, the plate, the printed 90 mm board, one M3 x 16 screw + M3 nut (pinch), two M3 x 8 countersunk
screws (plate; they self-tap into 2.6 mm pilot holes in the flange).

1. Print the clamp as is (z up, no supports), PETG or PLA, 100 % infill; the plate flat, countersinks up.
2. Screw the plate to the clamp's flange (countersunk screws flush), then glue the board over the plate's front
   face, aligned with its edges; the screw heads sit under the paper.
3. Open the gripper fully. Slide the clamp down over the fixed jaw from its tip until it seats (the cavity follows
   the jaw and wedges where the jaw widens), with the flange on the outer face, away from the moving jaw. Put the nut
   in its pocket and tighten the pinch screw across the ears until the clamp cannot be wiggled. Optionally a dab of
   hot glue between clamp and jaw.
4. Keep the gripper open during the calibration (the ears and the clamp's inner wall sit where the moving jaw
   closes). The board faces away from the moving jaw, 5 mm clear of the gripper body; its exact position on the
   gripper does not matter, the calibration solves for it.

Rigidity check: at the end of the calibration run, return to the first pose; the board pose seen by the camera
should repeat within about 0.5 mm and 0.2 deg.

Geometry: the cavity at each height is the union of every jaw cross-section above it, grown by 0.25 mm, so the clamp
slides on from the tip; built from `../prints/Wrist_Roll_Follower_SO101.stl` (sleeve band z = 55-72 mm in that
STL's frame). Untested on hardware: if the fit is too tight or loose, change `CLEARANCE` and regenerate.
